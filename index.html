I.   The invitation

Most teachings ask you to believe them.

This one asks the opposite.

Ehipassiko. Do not believe me. Come, and see for yourself.

So this chapter will not tell you what is true. It will hand you small things to look at, and let you find out.

II.   The first looking

These two squares. The left one is darker than the right.

You could take my word for it.

left
right
— or drag them together —

They are the same colour. They always were.

You almost believed me. That is how easily being told replaces seeing.

III.   The second looking

I say your attention is yours to place. Don't believe that either.

Press, and rest your attention on this — for one breath.

Did it stay? Did it wander?

Either way — you just watched your own attention. No one could have told you what you found.

IV.   The third looking

There is a woman the village speaks of in low voices. They say she had something to do with a death, years ago.

You have been told. Now — look.

You saw a woman draw water.

That is what you saw. That is all you saw. What you were told is not in the bucket, not in her hands, not in her face. Looking did not deliver it.

V.   What the three lookings teach
Looking gives you what looking can give you.
No more. No less.
Knowing the difference — that is the whole of the practice.
This was Chapter One.
Every chapter in Lucida will work this way.
Something will be said.
You will test it.
You will keep what survives.
Ehipassiko. Come, and see.
